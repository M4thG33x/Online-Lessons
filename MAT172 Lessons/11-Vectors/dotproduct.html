Dot Product

To wrap up this lesson, let's look at one more operation between two vectors, the vector dot product. We have already seen how to add or subtract two vectors, or multiply a vector by a scalar. But how do we multiply two vectors together? While it won't seem to be the obvious choice, the easiest method is to multiply the corresponing components and then add the results together. This means the dot product will actually result in a scalar value, not another vector. Let's look at a more formal definition.

Given two vectors \(\vec{u} = \langle a_1,a_2 \rangle\) and \(\vec{v} = \langle b_1,b_2 \rangle\), their dot product is scalar given by the following equation.

\[\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2\]

Example: Compute the dot product for \(\vec{u} = \langle -4,7\rangle\) and \(\vec{v} = \langle 11,5 \rangle\).

Muliplty the corresponding compoents and add the results together.

\[ \begin{aligned} \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} &= \langle \textcolor{#0066CC}{-4},\textcolor{#D95400}{7} \rangle \cdot \langle \textcolor{#0066CC}{11},\textcolor{#D95400}{5} \rangle \\[4pt] &= (\textcolor{#0066CC}{-4})(\textcolor{#0066CC}{11}) + (\textcolor{#D95400}{7})(\textcolor{#D95400}{5}) \\[4pt] &= -44 + 35\\[4pt] &= -9 \end{aligned} \]

The dot product of \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) is \(-9\).

An important relationship of the dot product is that it gives us a way to determine the angle between two vectors.

For nonzero vectors \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) separated by an angle \(\theta\), where \(0 \le \theta \le 180^{\circ}\), the dot product of \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) is related to \(\theta\) by the following ewquation.

\[\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = |\vec{u}||\vec{v}|\cos\theta\]

Things to consider:

  • If \(\vec{u} = \vec{0}\) or \(\vec{v} = \vec{0}\), then \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0\) and \(\theta\) is undefined.
  • The vectors \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) are parallel if \(\theta = 0^{\circ}\) or \(\theta = 180^{\circ}\).
  • The vectors \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) are perpendicular (\(\theta = 90^{\circ}\)) if and only if \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0\).
