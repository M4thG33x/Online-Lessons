On this page, you will learn to:

  • Evaluate surface integrals and flux using the Divergence Theorem.

We have already seen how Green's Theorem and Stoke's Theorem provide ways to connect the circulation of a vector field along a curve to the rotation of the field within the region or surface bounded by the curve. Can we do something similar with the flux surface integral?

\[\underset{S}{\iint} \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{S}\]

In other words, do we have a relationship between the flux of a field across a closed surface (or the outer surface of a solid) with the divergence of the field within the space bounded by the surface (inside the solid)? This is exactly what the Divergence Theorem gives us.

Let \(D\) be a simple solid region in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) with a boundary surface \(S\) having a positive (outward) orientation. Let \(\vec{F}\) be a vector field whose component functions have continuous partial first derivatives on an open region containing \(D\).

\[\underset{S}{\mathop \iint} \, \vec{F} \cdot \, d\vec{S} = \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, \text{div} \, \vec{F} \, dV\]

In other words, the total divergence of a vector field inside a solid region is equal to the divergence of the field across the boundary surface of the solid.
