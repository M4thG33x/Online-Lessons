• Mass & Inertia

On this page, you will learn to:

  • Solve applications involving triple integrals.

We can use triple integrals to solve many applications that are similar to those we discussed using double integrals. The primary difference is that we usually consider lamina regions with double integrals but we can now look at 3D solids, surfaces, and shapes with triple integrals. The formulas for these applications are similar to their corresponding double integral formulas, just with an added dimension. For example, double integrals of mass involve an area density while triple integrals of mass now work with the typical volume density function.

Given a 3-dimensional solid \(D\) with continuous density function \(\delta (x,y,z) \), the formulas for mass, moments of mass, and center of mass are given below. Notice that the moments of mass now describe the tendency for an object to rotate about a coordinate plane.

Mass in 3D
Mass Moments of Mass Center of Mass
\[m = \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, \delta (x,y,z) \, dV\] \[ \begin{aligned} M_{yz} &= \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, x \, \delta (x,y,z) \, dV \\[4pt] M_{xz} &= \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, y \, \delta (x,y,z) \, dV \\[4pt] M_{xy} &= \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, z \, \delta (x,y,z) \, dV \end{aligned} \] \[\left( \bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z} \right) = \left(\frac{M_{yz}}{m},\frac{M_{xz}}{m},\frac{M_{xy}}{m}\right) \]

We can also compute moments of inertia around the coordinate plane using the following formulas. Notice the similarities between these and the formulas that we use for inertia in 2D space about the axes and the origin. Note that some formulas use \(\delta\) for density while other formulas use \(\rho\), but it doesn't really matter which letter is used. Having said that, you may not want to use \(\rho\) for density when using spherical coordinates since \(\rho\) is a coordinate variable in the spherical system.

Moments of Inertia in 3D
About the \(x\)-Axis About the \(y\)-Axis About the \(z\)-Axis
\[I_x = \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, (y^2+z^2) \rho (x,y,z) \, dV\] \[I_y = \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, (x^2+z^2) \rho (x,y,z) \, dV\] \[I_z = \underset{D}{\mathop \iiint} \, (x^2+y^2) \rho (x,y,z) \, dV\]

We can also use the cylindrical and spherical coordinate systems to determine the mass, moments of mass and inertia, and center of mass. However, be aware that the formulas for center of mass still result in rectangular coordinates, \(\left( \bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z} \right)\). Using the cylindrical and spherical systems only transforms the interior of the integrals, its boundaries and density function, not the output of the integrals.
